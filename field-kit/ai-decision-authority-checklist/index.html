AI Decision Authority Checklist

Use this before an AI-assisted QA artifact becomes release evidence. The goal is to keep source facts, machine suggestions, human judgment, and lifecycle outcomes separate enough that someone can defend the decision later.

Download fillable HTML Read the post this came from →

How to use it

This page is the quick read. Download the fillable version when an AI-generated test plan, failure summary, risk note, release recommendation, or exception language is about to influence a real decision.

Step 1Start with source facts

Name the logs, test results, requirements, defects, telemetry, screenshots, or records the AI output used.

Step 2Separate the machine suggestion

Capture what AI inferred, grouped, summarized, classified, or proposed. Do not let the suggestion blur into the source facts.

Step 3Name the human judgment

Record who accepted, rejected, corrected, or left the output ambiguous, and what decision they own.

Step 4Control the outcome

Put the result where it belongs: defect, test, backlog item, risk record, release note, exception, or gate decision.

Use it for

Generated test casesConfirm the risk, source requirement, missing edge cases, and human owner.
Failure triageSeparate product defects, test defects, data issues, environment gaps, and accepted noise.
Release-risk summariesMake sure the clean language still carries uncertainty and action.
Exception languageName who accepted the risk, why, and when it needs to be revisited.

The gut-check line

If the human owner could not defend the AI-assisted output in release triage, it is not decision-grade yet.

Watch out

Do not use the checklist to rubber-stamp polished output. Use it to find where authority, evidence, or lifecycle control is missing.
